## Documented knowledge

### CTRL-01 — Proposed layered release validation

The release-process design aims to reduce conflicting READY actions and replace tactical controls with strategic validation.
A planned last-mile gate reconciles payment amount before outbound SWIFT publication.
The January 2026 discussion proposes validating current state as well as whether an action is allowed.
Workflow publication requires `READY+NA+NA`; SWIFT generation requires `READY+NA+PendingAck`.
The design separates cache locking, state transitions, duplicate detection, event grouping, and SWIFT duplicate checks.
Proposed keys include cashflow ID with business/minor version for workflow duplicates, tracking ID for message bridging, and cashflow ID/business version for SWIFT.
These source-described controls are target design context only. They supply no inspected implementation or execution evidence.

### CTRL-02 — Detailed READY-state transitions and concurrency scope

The detailed matrix distinguishes READY with NA, PendingException, and PendingAck substatus types.
READY/NA actions include return to operator/checker, hold, suppression, fail, withdrawal, un-net/un-split, net/split, utilization, PastDue, and technical requeue.
Full/auto utilization produces UTILIZED; partial utilization produces PARTIALLY_UTILIZED; no payment is generated by those actions.
PendingAck release and settle move to RELEASED and SETTLED. Regeneration/resend retains READY/PendingAck.
PendingAck withdrawal requeues and is identified as requiring further assessment.
The design lists auto-release every 30 minutes, auto-fail at `21:00 GMT`, and materialization at `02:00 GMT`.
It identifies unresolved SSI/confirmation, manual-action, and system-job interactions. Blank cells and question marks are not approvals or guaranteed conflict avoidance.
The January note restricts READY netting/splitting near cutoff; later appendix wording requires reconciliation.

### CTRL-03 — Economic and key-field amendment classification

The example table distinguishes economic change, key-field change, manual touch, and released state.
Economic changes produce Amendment in its examples.
Unchanged economics before manual touch/release can produce NonEcoAmend_Replace. A changed swap-agent key can also produce that replacement classification.
The table asks whether to check key fields or touch/release first; it does not settle a complete priority order.
Refixing examples require cashflow-only comparison when trade major version is 1. Economic cashflow change then produces Amendment.
For major version above 1 with no previous group, the note also classifies Amendment.
A dedicatedChange note routes that case to no-pair first. Its scope and precedence remain unresolved.

### CTRL-04 — October release-control intent

The October note retains two distinct intended controls: a near-cutoff net/split restriction and a READY-only downstream gate.
Its appendix allows Maker Submit and Checker Reject back to WAITING; Early Release remains READY.
Un-net and UnSplit produce DEAD; Hold produces HOLD. Manual suppression or fail enters WAITING for checker handling.
Full and partial utilization produce UTILIZED and PARTIALLY_UTILIZED respectively, with no generated payment.
Net and Split produce NETTED and SPLIT. Resend To Razor and Regenerate Swift remain READY.
The governing diagram says downstream release must stop whenever the cashflow moves away from READY.
The text mixes source-reported completed changes with proposed review scope. This candidate retains the intended predicates only.
